# Video Walkthrough Script (Approximately 2–3 Minutes)

**0:00–0:15 — Introduction**  
"This is the CAPSTONE 66 AI Data Storytelling Dashboard. It explores public CO₂
observations and separates reproducible calculations from optional generative AI."

**0:15–0:35 — Source and dataset**  
Show the About & Sources page, then Dataset & Cleaning.  
"The dashboard uses the Our World in Data CO₂ dataset. This screen reports the
actual source, columns, rows, missing values, duplicates, and cleaning operations.
The original CSV is preserved. Missing measurements are not replaced with zero."

**0:35–0:55 — Analytical questions**  
Open Question Generator.  
"Questions are generated from the dataset schema and grouped by trends, comparisons,
distributions, and relationships. These default questions are rule-based. Gemini
suggestions are optional and clearly identified as AI-generated."

**0:55–1:25 — Dashboard**  
Open Overview. Change year range and select a country or two.  
"The dashboard shows the available-year coverage, the selected measure, an annual
trend, and the leading country-level contributors. OWID aggregate rows are excluded
from country totals. The chart responds to the selected filters."

**1:25–1:50 — Story and verification**  
Open Data Story, then Number Verification.  
"The story translates calculated observations into plain language without claiming
causation. Verification independently checks totals, rankings, year-over-year
changes, and the chart aggregation."

**1:50–2:10 — Notes and prompts**  
Open Insight Notes and Prompt Library.  
"A finding can be saved with its source and exported. The prompt library supports
responsible follow-up analysis and presentation preparation."

**2:10–2:25 — Responsible AI and conclusion**  
"AI-generated explanations are optional and require a configured API key. They
remain unverified until reviewed by a person. Results describe the available data;
they do not establish why emissions changed. Thank you."

**Presenter checklist:** Run the app before recording, use the actual dataset values,
avoid showing credentials, and do not claim to have used tools or created evidence
that you have not actually used or created.
